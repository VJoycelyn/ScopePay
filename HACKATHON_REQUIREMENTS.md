# PayPal AI Hackathon — verified requirements

Verified from the official Devpost and PayPal pages on October 20, 2026. This is the PayPal AI Hackathon, not the OpenCV competition.

## What the entry must do

Build a new application, or significantly improve an existing one during the hackathon, using the PayPal developer platform in its free sandbox environment and an AI tool, model, or platform. PayPal must be central to the project, and AI must meaningfully affect its functionality. There are no prescribed problem statements or fixed tracks. Solo participation is allowed. Sponsor tools are optional. [1] [2] [3]

The final application must actually work as described and shown. A static mockup or non-functional prototype is not sufficient. Judges must be able to run it from complete repository instructions or interact with a hosted demo. [1]

## Deadline and access window

Submissions close **November 12, 2026, at 12:00 p.m. Pacific Standard Time**, which is **4:00 p.m. Atlantic Standard Time** and **8:00 p.m. UTC**. Submit before the deadline rather than relying on last-minute upload completion. Judging runs December 1–15, 2026; winners are expected around December 21. Keep the application available to judges free of charge and without restrictions through the end of judging. [1]

## Required submission materials

- A working application that meets the PayPal and AI requirements.
- An English text description of its functionality and the tools used.
- A public GitHub, GitLab, or Bitbucket repository containing all necessary source code, assets, setup/run instructions, and a detectable open-source license.
- A hosted functional demo or clear, complete instructions that let judges run the application themselves.
- A publicly visible YouTube video under three minutes showing the actual application working on its intended device. Do not use third-party copyrighted material or trademarks without permission. [1] [2]

Protect all merchant secrets, AI API keys, real payment credentials, and personal data. Provide safe judge-access instructions rather than committing private credentials to a public repository. Label sandbox payments, sample data, and any unverified capabilities clearly.

## Judging priorities

**Technological Implementation:** skillful, substantial integration of the PayPal platform and AI, demonstrated through a genuinely working implementation.

**Design:** a coherent, complete product experience, rather than a disconnected proof of concept.

**Potential Impact:** a credible and specific audience, problem, and demonstrated solution.

**Innovation/Idea:** a creative concept with meaningful differentiation.

**Presentation:** a clear end-to-end demonstration that explains the problem, audience, and value. The controlling Official Rules state that these five criteria are **equally weighted**. Stage One is a pass/fail check of theme and required API/SDK fit. [1] [2]

## Prize strategy

The overall first prize is $12,000. Honorable mentions include Most Creative, Most Impactful, Best Demo Delivery, Best Use of PayPal + AI, and Best Use of Agentic Commerce, each listed as $5,000. A single entry can receive one Grand Prize plus one Sponsor Prize, or one Honorable Mention plus one Sponsor Prize—not multiple main awards. Sponsor integration should add actual value rather than complexity for its own sake. No project can be guaranteed to win. [1]

The overview currently lists $67,500+ in prizes; other official material refers to $60,000+, and the screenshot displays $69,750. Use the official prize-category rules for specific awards rather than pitching an assumed total. [1] [2] [4]

## Eligibility and ownership

Entrants must be at least the age of majority where they live. Every team member must satisfy eligibility requirements. Certain jurisdictions are excluded, including Brazil, Quebec, Russia, Crimea, Cuba, Iran, and North Korea, as well as comprehensively sanctioned jurisdictions. Registration alone does not establish eligibility. Review the full official rules for any applicable employment, conflict-of-interest, or jurisdiction exclusions. [1] [3]

The submission must be the entrant's original work and must not infringe third-party rights. Disclose AI services, AI coding assistance, libraries, assets, and any prior work accurately. Do not claim user research, customer outcomes, security guarantees, benchmarks, or completed integrations that have not been demonstrated.
